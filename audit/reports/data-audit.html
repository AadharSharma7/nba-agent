A useful foundation, with material coverage limits

Kaggle v515 can support a focused NBA statistics prototype. It cannot, by itself, support a reliable promise of complete NBA history or unrestricted statistical answers.

Recommendation: proceed with a curated subset. Keep the original snapshot, normalize structured records into PostgreSQL later, and expose only metrics and scopes that pass coverage checks. Do not embed box scores or use vector search for statistical calculations.

Your stated tolerance is small gaps, but not large ones. The known 2024–25 game-level issues affect 7 of 1,230 scheduled regular-season games (about 0.57%); this is compatible with a prototype that discloses incomplete scopes. It is not a bound on every possible player/metric defect. The early-history omissions are substantial and do not meet that tolerance.

What the priority questions prove

Curry’s 2016 Western Conference Finals

Seven games produce 195 points, 44 rebounds and 41 assists: 27.9 PPG, 6.3 RPG and 5.9 APG. Shooting totals are 62/140 FG, 32/77 3PT and 39/44 FT. These match the external StatMuse reference. Percentages must be computed from summed makes and attempts, not by averaging game percentages.

Minutes do not pass the same check. The source totals 254 minutes, implying 36.3 MPG; the reference displays 36.7 MPG. Pre-2024–25 minutes are overwhelmingly integer values. The audit establishes lost precision, not the exact upstream rounding algorithm. Suppress or repair MPG and per-minute metrics before presenting them as accurate.

Material defects and their implications

  1. Early NBA coverage is substantially incomplete. The 1949–50 regular-season table contains 122 games involving eight teams, while the NBA historical source identifies 17 teams. A date range reaching 1949 does not mean complete coverage. Do not advertise complete all-time totals or leaderboards.
  2. Recent seasons also need reconciliation. The 2024–25 schedule has 1,230 regular games; the game table contains 1,225. Five schedule IDs are absent, and two additional stored games have 0–0 scores and no player rows. Schedule timestamps may be stale after rescheduling, so use them to identify gaps, not as authoritative played dates.
  3. Missing information is often encoded as zero. There are 85,156 NBA player rows with zero minutes and positive points. Many historical attempts and rebound splits are also inconsistent with recorded makes or totals. Never globally replace zero with NULL or treat every zero as measured data; define availability by metric, era and record evidence.
  4. Game and team errors need quarantine. Four stored games end in tied scores, including the two 0–0 records. Two 2025–26 games have an extra team-stat row with team ID 0. There are 739 team/game point-total mismatches despite non-null player points; missing players and source errors remain possible.
  5. Identity and date joins need explicit rules. 48,430 in-scope player rows lack source team IDs but can be mapped through game ID plus home/away. Nine non-null team assignments conflict with that mapping, and eleven player timestamps disagree with game timestamps. Retain provenance and flag conflicts rather than silently trusting either side.
  6. DNP comments are not a complete availability history. Five rows have positive minutes alongside DNP/inactive-like comments. Absence of a player row does not establish injury, team membership or unavailability. Historical rosters/transactions and a participation policy are prerequisites for robust “team without player” answers.

There are no duplicate observed game keys or player/game keys. Every NBA-scope player row joins to a player biography. The 4,906 source player rows without a game occur outside the selected regular/playoff scope, in preseason or All-Star data. These reassuring checks do not prove game or player completeness.

Play-by-play and advanced data

Play-by-play contains 18,727,295 events across 39,164 games, starting in 1996. The observed (game ID, action number) key has no duplicates. Its 89-column schema combines multiple generations of data. Looking only at the primary date column would incorrectly suggest an April 2026 cutoff: an alternate date field extends through June 2026, and 56,192 events lack both dates. Join canonical games and validate events before use.

Keep play-by-play as a raw Parquet asset initially. Box scores suffice for the current examples. Possessions, lineup stints, defensive matchups and on/off statistics need separate reconstruction and validation. The presence of an advanced-rating column does not validate its definition or justify averaging ratings across games.

Storage and agent implications

Use three layers: unchanged raw files with checksums; normalized relational data; and a coverage/quality record attached to every answerable scope. Statistical answers should be computed through typed Python tools over relational data. Vector embeddings add no value for summing box scores or identifying shared games.

The Analyst’s knowledge tool should return data plus player/game IDs, filters, denominator, metric definitions, source version, cutoff, coverage status and exclusions. The LLM can write the explanation, but cannot upgrade incomplete evidence into a complete claim. Document retrieval can be added separately for rules/history; no vector database is necessary for this audit or first statistical milestone.

Recommended ingestion behavior: retain source values; classify phases; normalize IDs, dates and series labels; derive missing team IDs only with recorded evidence; quarantine conflicting records; and expose coverage-aware query results. No corrections or PostgreSQL ingestion have been performed in this audit.

Audit method, reproducibility and limits

All ten raw files were downloaded from pinned version 515, extracted with ZIP CRC validation and SHA-256 hashing, and inspected in full for row counts and relevant keys. The audit ran 52 SQL checks without execution errors. Source defects above are findings, not passing data-quality tests. A separate standard-library CSV pass reproduced the priority aggregates, and all raw-file hashes still match.

External verification is a targeted spot-check, not certification of every season, game, metric or play-by-play event. The snapshot’s own schedule is useful internal evidence; historical gap estimates require further independent schedules. The report does not validate the full meaning of all 110 advanced player columns or establish commercial rights.
